## The Attack

Have you ever received _spam_? I know I have. Quite a lot, actually.

One message in particular caught my attention a few days ago: it claimed to be a **Brazilian electronic invoice (NF-e)**. Given its formatting, it could have fooled me.

It also instructed me to extract the file and view it in my browser.

> Alright, this is getting weird...

And, well, it wasn't in the SPAM folder for nothing. Outlook did a good job filtering that email.

The link went through a few pages before reaching an “Open document” button. Clicking it opened a search in Windows Explorer for ZIP files in a WebDAV folder controlled by the attacker. The `search-ms:` protocol can open this kind of search, and the window appeared with the name `Downloads`.

So, basically, the folder the victim saw was on a server controlled by the attacker, pretending to be the computer's `Downloads` folder.

Inside the ZIP was a VBScript (`.vbs`) with a name that looked like a PDF invoice. When run, it downloaded and started other components in the chain, obfuscated so they would not be easily detected.

The final program had functions to stream the screen, control the keyboard and mouse, collect contacts, and resend emails. In the end, the invoice was just bait: the goal was to install a remote-access program.

## Conclusion

**Phishing** is a social-engineering technique widely used today to trick people into compromising their personal or professional security.

As we saw here, a convincing email could lead to a catastrophic security incident, such as leaked credentials, stolen sensitive information, and compromised corporate systems.

Always be wary of unexpected emails, suspicious links, and attachments from unknown senders. Caution is the best defense against phishing attacks and malware.
